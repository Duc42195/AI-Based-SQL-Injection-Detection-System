| comment_12  | Strong aspects                          |  N/A  | The paper presents a novel two-branch query-level detection architecture at the database proxy that effectively combines supervised multi-class classification (DistilBERT) with benign-only anomaly detection (Local Outlier Factor). Its primary strength lies in its rigorous leave-one-attack-family-out evaluation methodology, which exposes how closed-set classification performance (where DistilBERT achieves a 0.9699 macro-F1) can mask generalization failures on unseen attack families. Furthermore, the authors implement a campaign-aware data split across four heterogeneous public datasets to eliminate near-duplicate leakage, proving that benign-only structural anomaly detection provides critical complementary coverage by recovering stealthy attacks, raising unseen boolean-blind SQLi detection from 13.5% up to 88.8% combined coverage. |
| comment_12a | Weak aspects                            |  N/A  | The proposed system exhibits notable generalization blind spots and operational overheads. The supervised branch demonstrates a severe vulnerability to unseen boolean-blind queries, mapping 86.5% of them to normal traffic, leaving an 11.2% residual undetected gap even after anomaly recovery. Operationally, LOF carries a 5.27% false-positive rate and adds an 18–21 ms per-query latency budget, which may cause friction or delays in high-throughput production proxies. Finally, the study suffers from scope and evaluation constraints, as it relies on extracted HTTP parameters rather than live native proxy logs, contains an estimated 13% label noise in boolean-blind data, and omits session-level probe correlation, out-of-band SQLi, and adversarial evasion techniques. |
